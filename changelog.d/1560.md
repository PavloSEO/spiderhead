- Sitemap extensions are parsed and retained instead of silently dropped. `image:image`
  (loc, title, caption, geo_location, license), `news:news` (name — including the
  `<news:publication><news:name>` nesting — publication_date, title), `video:video`
  (thumbnail_loc, title, duration, family_friendly and the other simple child tags) and
  `xhtml:link rel="alternate" hreflang` alternates now ride along on their `<url>` entry,
  with relative `image:loc` and `href` values resolved like page locations. Entries without
  extensions keep the exact `{loc, lastmod, changefreq, priority}` shape they always had.
- Pages and attachments are counted separately. The sitemap crawler reports `loc_entries`
  beside `image_entries`, `news_entries` and `video_entries`, and the SF coverage summary
  carries `image_entries_in_sitemap` / `news_entries_in_sitemap` / `video_entries_in_sitemap`
  plus an "Extension entries in sitemap" line in the markdown report. The counters appear
  only when the sitemap declared such entries — a plain sitemap reports no measured zero.
- A crawl-versus-sitemap comparison no longer reports the sitemap's own images as missing.
  The real case: a Yoast sitemap with 1,283 `<loc>` and 1,130 `<image:loc>` entries had
  crawl-found webp files flagged "not in sitemap" although the sitemap declared them for
  their pages, because the comparison saw `<loc>` entries only. Reconciliation now accepts
  the declared image locations as a separate asset population: such URLs leave the
  "not in sitemap" and "not comparable" buckets and are returned as their own
  `linked_assets_in_sitemap` list with the declared total under `asset_entries_in_sitemap`.
  Omitting the new argument keeps the historical output shape.
- Sitemap-index nesting of any depth is followed and pinned by test — the walk is bounded by
  the already-visited set, so an index cycle terminates and the document cap still bounds
  the traversal. `lastmod` values of every W3C precision (date, datetime, timezone offset,
  RFC3339) are retained verbatim instead of being normalised away, a BOM no longer leaks
  into the first URL of a text sitemap, and elements are matched on local names, so
  non-standard or mistyped `xmlns` URIs and undeclared extension prefixes still parse.
